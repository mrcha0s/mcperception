# Presentation Design Guide

Based on **"20 Tips for Better Presentation Design"** by Taras Bakusevych (UX Collective).

Reference: https://uxdesign.cc/20-tips-for-better-presentation-design-24950311d98c

---

## 1. Define the Goal First

> The first question before creating any presentation is "What is the goal?"

- A clearly defined presentation objective is the **first step** to a remarkable presentation
- Every slide must serve this goal — remove anything that doesn't
- Ask: What should the audience think, feel, or do after this presentation?

---

## 2. Know Your Audience

- Understand the **interests and motivations** of the primary audience
- Create a sharper, more relevant **targeted message**
- Create the story with this specific person/group in mind
- Frame your offer for them — not for you

---

## 3. Use Storytelling

Storytelling forges a **powerful connection** with the audience, keeping them invested and engaged. The story climax should counterbalance the point of lowest attention.

### StoryBrand Framework (Donald Miller)
Every presentation story needs these elements:

| Element | What It Represents |
|---------|-------------------|
| **Hero** | Customer, user, organization, team |
| **Problem** | Challenges, pain points, risks |
| **Guide** | Product, company, or service |
| **Journey** | Vision and concrete plan to solve the problem |
| **Success** | Bright future and transformed hero |

---

## 4. Manage Attention Spans

- For longer presentations: create **breaks or format changes every 20 minutes** to recharge audience attention
- Use **break/transition slides** to indicate moving from one part to another
- These slides help both audience and presenter catch a breath

---

## 5. The 8-Second Rule

> If the information on your slide cannot be consumed in under 8 seconds, there is a high chance it will not be consumed fully.

- Your presentation is **not a book** — it should be the most efficient way to communicate a point
- Every slide must pass the 8-second scan test
- If a slide takes longer to read, it needs to be simplified or split

---

## 6. Reduce Text, Use Visual Aids

- Reduce text and use **visual aids** for support to keep audience focus
- Replace paragraphs with diagrams, charts, icons, images
- If you cannot further trim copy, use **font hierarchy** to highlight important messages and clearly communicate content structure

### Text Hierarchy Rules
- **Title**: Large, bold — the main takeaway
- **Subtitle/Key Point**: Medium weight — supporting context
- **Body/Detail**: Smaller — only if absolutely necessary
- Use no more than **3 levels** of hierarchy per slide

---

## 7. Embrace White Space

> Whitespace is one of the most overlooked elements that make up a great presentation layout.

- Don't fill every pixel — let content breathe
- White space creates focus, guides the eye, and communicates professionalism
- More white space = more perceived quality and clarity

---

## 8. Use Genuine, High-Quality Images

- Good images are **full of emotions and tell real stories**
- Avoid cheesy stock photos — they undermine credibility
- Use resources like **Unsplash** that host curated royalty-free images
- Images should support the narrative, not decorate

---

## 9. Match Visual Style to Context

Many factors influence presentation visual style:
- **Topic** — technical vs creative vs business
- **Audience** — executives vs developers vs general public
- **Branding** — company colors, fonts, identity

> Make sure you are not too serious or too childish when you don't need to be.

---

## 10. Be Consistent Throughout

- Stick to **consistent styles** for fonts, icons, colors, and illustrations throughout the whole presentation
- Define key slide templates (**Master/Template file**) to enforce consistency
- Consistency = professionalism and trust

### Consistency Checklist
- [ ] Same font family across all slides
- [ ] Same color palette (max 3-5 colors)
- [ ] Same icon style (outline OR filled, not mixed)
- [ ] Same illustration style
- [ ] Consistent spacing and margins
- [ ] Consistent heading sizes and positions

---

## 11. Use Break/Transition Slides

- Break slides indicate a **transition** from one section to another
- They help the audience reset and prepare for new content
- Use a distinct visual treatment: section title on a bold/dark background, minimal text

---

## 12. Use Data Visualization Effectively

- Replace raw numbers with **charts, graphs, and infographics**
- Choose the right chart type (bar for comparison, line for trends, pie only for simple proportions)
- Keep charts simple — one key insight per chart
- Label data directly on the chart

---

## 13. Use Product Screenshots and Mockups

- Show the actual product in realistic **device mockups**
- Screenshots make abstract features concrete
- Use consistent mockup styles (same device frame, same angle)
- Crop to highlight the relevant area — don't show a full screen if only one section matters

---

## 14. Use Iconography

- Icons help communicate concepts **quickly** without text
- Use a consistent icon set (same style, weight, and size)
- Icons should **supplement** text, not replace critical information
- Stick to universally recognizable icons

---

## 15. Use Animations Purposefully

- Animations should **guide attention**, not distract
- Use subtle transitions: fade, slide — not bounce, spin, or fly
- Animate to reveal information progressively (build slides)
- ❌ Never use animations just for decoration

---

## 16. Use Strong Contrast

- Ensure text is **highly readable** against backgrounds
- Dark text on light background OR light text on dark background
- Test contrast ratios — minimum 4.5:1 for body text
- Use contrast to create visual hierarchy: high contrast for primary, lower for secondary

---

## 17. Use a Defined Color Palette

- Define your palette **before** starting design
- Max **3-5 colors**: 1 primary, 1-2 secondary, 1-2 accent
- Use the **60-30-10 rule**: 60% dominant, 30% secondary, 10% accent
- Ensure brand alignment

---

## 18. Include a Clear Call to Action

- Every presentation should end with a **clear next step**
- Tell the audience exactly what to do: sign up, contact, visit, schedule, approve
- Make the CTA slide visually distinct and memorable
- Include contact information or a link

---

## 19. Review and Rehearse

- Review every slide for **clarity, consistency, and accuracy**
- Rehearse the presentation to check **flow and timing**
- Get feedback from someone who hasn't seen it before
- Test on the actual display/projector if possible

---

## 20. Less Is More

> Your presentation is not a book.

- One **key message** per slide
- If a slide tries to say too much, split it into two
- Remove any element that doesn't directly support the slide's message
- Simpler slides are more memorable and more impactful

---

## Slide Type Templates

| Slide Type | Purpose | Layout Guidance |
|------------|---------|----------------|
| **Title** | Opening slide | Large title, subtitle, speaker name, date. Bold, clean. |
| **Agenda/Overview** | Show structure | 3-5 bullet points or numbered items. Minimal detail. |
| **Section Break** | Transition between parts | Section title centered on bold/dark background. |
| **Content** | Key information | Title + 1 visual OR title + 3 short bullet points max. |
| **Data/Chart** | Show metrics | Single chart with title and 1-line insight callout. |
| **Quote** | Emphasis/credibility | Large quote text, attribution, minimal background. |
| **Screenshot/Demo** | Show product | Device mockup with callout annotations. |
| **Comparison** | Side-by-side | Two columns, clear labels, visual differentiation. |
| **CTA/Closing** | Next steps | Clear action, contact info, bold visual. |
| **Thank You** | End | Simple, branded, with contact details. |

---

## Implementation Checklist

Before finalizing any presentation, verify:

- [ ] Goal is clearly defined — every slide serves it
- [ ] Audience is identified — content is framed for them
- [ ] Story structure is in place (Hero → Problem → Guide → Journey → Success)
- [ ] Every slide passes the **8-second rule**
- [ ] Text is minimized — visual aids used wherever possible
- [ ] Font hierarchy is clear (max 3 levels)
- [ ] White space is generous — slides are not cramped
- [ ] Images are genuine and high-quality (no cheesy stock photos)
- [ ] Visual style matches topic, audience, and brand
- [ ] Fonts, colors, icons, and illustrations are **consistent** throughout
- [ ] Break/transition slides separate major sections
- [ ] Data is visualized (not raw numbers in text)
- [ ] Product is shown in mockups where relevant
- [ ] Icons are from a consistent set
- [ ] Animations are purposeful and subtle (or absent)
- [ ] Contrast is strong — text is highly readable
- [ ] Color palette follows 60-30-10 rule (max 3-5 colors)
- [ ] Presentation ends with a **clear call to action**
- [ ] Content has been reviewed for accuracy and flow
- [ ] Each slide has **one key message** only

## Quick Reference: Common Mistakes

| ❌ Don't | ✅ Do Instead |
|----------|--------------|
| Wall of text on a slide | One key message + visual aid |
| Cheesy stock photos | Genuine, emotional, curated images |
| Mixed icon styles (outline + filled + 3D) | One consistent icon style throughout |
| Bright text on bright background | Strong contrast (dark on light or light on dark) |
| Random colors per slide | Defined 3-5 color palette applied consistently |
| Fancy bounce/spin animations | Subtle fade/slide or no animation |
| No clear ending | Clear CTA slide with next steps |
| Too many points per slide | Split into multiple slides — one idea each |
| Reading slides word-for-word | Slides support the talk, not replace it |
| No section breaks in long presentations | Transition slides every 15-20 minutes |
